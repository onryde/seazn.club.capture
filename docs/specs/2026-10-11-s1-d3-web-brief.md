# S1 D3 — brief for the web (`mobile-s1-web`)

**From:** the capture app session (`mobile-s1`), 2026-10-11.
**Basis:** `docs/specs/2026-10-11-s1-d3-findings-design.md` §11. The rulings it
cites (R3, R4) are the owner's, dated 2026-10-11.

The app never sends a stream code's raw value or a token in a message. If the
web needs one, the owner gives it in person.

## 1. Record upload endpoint (new; owner rulings R3 and R4)

The operator taps _Send record to Seazn_. Nothing uploads by itself.

- **Endpoint.** `POST` under the capture API. Its path is the web's choice.
- **Authentication.** The code's token, sent the way beats send it.
- **Who may send.** The server accepts a record for any currently valid token
  of that code, including from a phone that the slot has replaced or that no
  longer holds the slot. The app keeps the token for 24 h after an unpair, for
  this one use.
- **Body.**
  - The scrubbed session record, `application/x-ndjson`, at most 1,000 lines and
    1 MB. `Content-Encoding: gzip` is accepted.
  - Each line already passes the shared scrub allow-list
    (`modules/capture-engine/src/scrub/allow-list.json`).
  - The server rejects, and never stores, a body with a field outside that list
    or a value shaped like a secret.
- **Answers.**
  - `201 {"ref":"R-7K2Q"}`: a short reference for the operator to read to the
    organiser.
  - `401` for a bad or expired token.
  - `413` for a body that is too large.
  - `429` when a per-code rate limit is hit (a proposal: 10 an hour).
- **Storage.**
  - Keyed to org, competition, fixture and slot, and to the session ids found
    in the record's lines.
  - A line is attached only to sessions of the code whose token sent it; the
    rest are dropped. The record is the phone's last 1,000 lines across codes,
    so a phone used for one club and then another sends the first club's lines
    under the second's token. The app does not filter them (the controller's
    ruling, 2026-10-12, on Minor 14 of the app's Batch 6 review).
  - Retention of 30 days is proposed; the owner's call.
  - Shown in the console next to the session, for org admins only.
- **Contract.** Publish a contract file for the endpoint. The app vendors it
  with `pnpm contracts:vendor` and pins it.
- **Published, 2026-10-12.** The owner approved the web's design on
  2026-10-12, and its contract merged as web #941 (`53f28ebfc`):
  `docs/contracts/capture-record.v1.json` and 52 fixtures, with the design
  `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-12-capture-record-upload-design.md` (web
  repo). The app vendored the contract at `53f28ebfc` (`contracts/`, pinned by
  `test/contractPins.test.ts`), and again at `55ac34d5d` (web #943, which
  serves the route on staging; Batch 12, ea636f2), and posts to it:
  - `POST /api/v1/capture/codes/{code}/records`, with the code's Bearer, at
    most 1,000 lines and 1,048,576 bytes, uncompressed;
  - a 201 is read for its `ref` alone, the only thing the app shows; `kept`
    and `dropped` are never read (RD, the owner's ruling of 2026-10-12). 401,
    413 and 429 are read by their status, and every other answer is a
    failure;
  - each line's `at` is the contract's (ISO 8601 with seconds and a zone), and
    `level` is optional, the mask included;
  - a field whose key the contract does not list is dropped from its line
    before the send, and the line goes (Q4, below);
  - a bad line is dropped by the server, never refused (the owner's ruling
    RL, in the contract since `55ac34d5d`): `422` is only a body the server
    cannot read as lines, which the app never sends, and reads as _Can't
    send this record_.

  The rules are `src/services/recordContract.ts`, each held to the vendored
  file by `recordContract.test.ts`; the port is
  `src/services/recordUploadPort.ts`, swept with every fixture.

- **On, 2026-10-12.** _Send record to Seazn_ is switched on in the app. The
  owner was asked in the session on 2026-10-12 whether to turn it on with the
  endpoint on staging, and chose "On now (Recommended)". A
  production build sends to production, and every send there answers
  _Can't send this record_, "Seazn doesn't take records here" (a 404), until
  production has the endpoint.

- **Five keys, added at `55ac34d5d`.** The contract's keys were the
  allow-list at capture `7ea8f63`, and the app had written five more since:
  `bytes` (`record.send`'s own), `failures`, `pickAspect16x9`, `pickSource`
  and `retryMs`. `55ac34d5d` lists them (the allow-list at capture
  `ebac190`), with `bytes`, `failures` and `retryMs` whole numbers of at
  least 0 and `pickAspect16x9` a boolean, as the app writes them. The app
  re-vendored, and its key pin now holds the contract's keys equal to every
  key it writes, so the upload drops none of its own.
- **Capture's answers to the web's §12.** The evidence, file by file, is in
  the batch 11 answers of the D3 fixes run:
  - **Q4, mask or drop: drop, for the upload only.** The checks that fail
    closed still run on each line as written: the token anywhere, or a raw
    string under an unlisted key, sends nothing. Then each unlisted field,
    a masked never key included, leaves its line, and the line goes. A line
    with nothing to drop goes byte for byte. Share record keeps its masks.
    The app does not narrow `sid` or `tokenTag`, or run S2–S5 itself: no
    writer of the app's breaks the first two, and since the owner's ruling
    (relayed by the web, 2026-10-12) the server drops a bad line and still
    answers 201, so a secret-shaped value or a value off its class costs that
    line, not the record. `SrtTarget.toString()` never reaches a line.
    No writer of a text key formats a target, and a target handed to the
    record as a value is masked whole (pinned in `VendorLinesTest`). A
    vendor's message quoting a held stream id or passphrase is masked in
    place, and trips no S3 (pinned there too).
  - **Q6, lines before the first `pair`: no app consequence that matters.**
    Those lines are the phone's own with no pairing: Home, Settings, an
    earlier send, a refused pair, which writes no `pair` line. A truncated
    first window loses its lines with no sid, and keeps its `sid` lines (A1).
    The app does not prepend a `pair` line. It no longer has the evicted
    one, and a made-up one could file another code's lines under the
    sending code.
  - **Q7, the kinds that end a pairing: `unpaired` and `ended`, with two
    exceptions.** `unpaired` covers re-pair, permissions, the operator's
    unpair, the notification's Forget (`forgotten`), and the server's
    `code-ended`, `replaced` and `taken`. `ended` covers a Stop from live and
    the fatal errors that go through `end()`. `broadcast-ended` ends a
    broadcast, not the pairing. The exceptions are two fatal paths into
    Ended that write neither kind: `pair-refused` over a held pairing
    (`Pairings.kt:14-16`) and `engine-error` (`Engine.kt:141`). Until the
    next `pair`, they file the same phone's later lines under the code. That
    is over-attribution, never a cross-code leak. Either native writes
    `ended` on both, or the web also closes a window at `reset`, which always
    comes before the next `pair`. The web chose its side at `55ac34d5d`: a
    window also closes at `pair-refused`, `engine-error` and `reset`, and
    before the next line the line schema refuses. Native is unchanged.
  - **Q8, the tag: final.** FNV-1a 32-bit over UTF-16 code units, as 8
    lowercase hex digits: `TokenTag.kt` and `tokenTag.ts`, which agree on the
    9 shared vectors in `modules/capture-engine/src/wire/token-tag-vectors.json`.
    The tag also names a pairing to JS's saved-code match and to the durable
    Forget marker, which is persisted across updates. A change would be a
    coordinated migration, and none is planned.

## 2. A question: the round line in other languages (N34)

In French the phone shows the round line as the server sends it: "ROUND 2",
"SF", "Final". Will the web localise it by the phone's language? If yes, the
beat or the descriptor would need to carry the language. Or does the phone keep
the server's English?
